# HR Employee Portal | Business Analysis & Requirements Management

## Project Overview

This project focuses on the business analysis, requirements definition and project planning for a proposed internal HR Employee Portal.

The aim was to design a centralised digital solution to improve HR processes, reduce manual administrative tasks and support communication between employees and HR departments.

The project covers the stages of requirements engineering and project planning, including stakeholder analysis, functional and non-functional requirements, acceptance criteria, scope definition, cost estimation, risk management and requirements traceability.

**Project type:** Business Analysis & Requirements Management  
**Project scope:** System requirements and implementation planning  
**Status:** Planning and documentation project (no software implementation)

## Business Problem

The proposed organisation relies on manual HR processes, emails and disconnected spreadsheets to manage employee requests, internal communication and HR documentation.

These processes can result in administrative inefficiencies, delayed approvals, inconsistent information and limited transparency.

The proposed HR Employee Portal aims to address these challenges through a centralised, browser-based platform.

## Proposed System Features

The following functionalities were included in the project scope:

- **Role-Based Authentication** – secure access for employees and HR staff.
- **Leave Management** – submission, tracking, approval and rejection of leave requests.
- **Internal Announcements** – publication and management of HR updates.
- **HR Document Repository** – centralised access to policies, forms and documents.
- **Employee Directory** – searchable employee information by name, department and job title.
- **Real-Time Messaging** – internal communication between employees and HR personnel.

## Business & System Requirements

Business requirements were identified and translated into functional and non-functional requirements.

### Functional Requirements

Examples include:

- Employees must be able to submit leave requests by selecting leave type and dates.
- HR staff must be able to approve or reject leave requests.
- HR staff must be able to upload and organise HR documents.
- Users must receive notifications about relevant updates.
- Employees must be able to search the employee directory.

### Non-Functional Requirements

The project defined measurable requirements relating to:

- **Performance:** Login and directory search response times.
- **Security:** HTTPS, role-based access and password requirements.
- **Availability:** Target of 99% uptime during business hours.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG accessibility guidelines.
- **Compatibility:** Support for modern web browsers.

### SMART Requirements

High-level requirements were refined into specific and measurable criteria.

Examples:

- Achieve at least a 98% successful login rate during testing.
- Deliver leave request notifications within two minutes.
- Return employee directory search results within two seconds.
- Provide access to at least ten downloadable HR documents.

## Acceptance Criteria

Acceptance criteria were defined for the main system functionalities to establish conditions for successful delivery and testing.

These covered:

- Successful authentication and role-based access.
- Leave submission, approval and notification workflows.
- Announcement publishing and chronological display.
- HR document accessibility and downloading.
- Employee directory search functionality.
- Secure and reliable internal messaging.

## Stakeholder Analysis

The project included identifying stakeholders, analysing their interests and defining responsibilities.

Stakeholder groups included:

- Executive Sponsor
- HR Director and HR Team
- Project Manager
- IT Department
- Employees
- Legal and Compliance Team

A stakeholder communication matrix was developed to define information requirements, communication channels and reporting frequency.

## Project Planning & Cost Estimation

A structured implementation plan was developed, covering a proposed **15-week project timeline**.

The plan included project initiation, requirements definition, design, development, testing, deployment and project closeout.

Two cost estimation approaches were applied:

- **Analogous Estimation** – comparison with a similar project.
- **Bottom-Up Estimation** – estimation based on tasks, resource allocation and hourly rates.

The project was planned against a budget of **€30,000**, including a contingency reserve.

A Gantt chart was prepared to illustrate tasks, dependencies, estimated hours and the project schedule.

## Risk & Quality Management

A risk analysis matrix was developed to assess potential challenges and define mitigation strategies.

Key risks included:

- System integration complexity
- Data privacy and security
- User resistance to new processes

The quality management approach included planned unit testing, integration testing, user acceptance testing (UAT), stakeholder reviews and quality criteria for major deliverables.

## Requirements Traceability & Validation

A requirements traceability matrix was developed to connect business requirements with stakeholder needs and project objectives.

The project also documented requirement refinements based on stakeholder feedback.

Examples included:

- Adding measurable login performance criteria.
- Expanding leave management requirements to include notifications and status tracking.
- Defining employee directory search performance requirements.
- Specifying document format and browser compatibility.

These activities demonstrate how requirements can be clarified, validated and managed throughout the project lifecycle.
